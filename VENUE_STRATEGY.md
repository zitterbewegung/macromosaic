# Publication strategy

## Primary target

**Journal of Applied and Computational Topology (JACT).** The manuscript's strongest fit is the intersection of a discrete topological representation, microscopic complexity constructions, and a signed-tangle decidability frontier. The paper should foreground the bounded NP-completeness theorem, the exact-core counting theorem, and the precise equivariant-embedding question left open for four or more strings.

## Strong alternatives

1. **Journal of Knot Theory and Its Ramifications.** Best if the framing emphasizes raw mosaic topology, the blank-moat soundness theorem, and the relative signed-tangle frontier.
2. **ACM Transactions on Computation Theory.** Best if the framing emphasizes the bounded NP theorem, parsimonious exact-core counting, and the distinction between bounded witnesses and existentially unbounded completion.
3. **Computational Complexity.** Appropriate but ambitious; the submission should emphasize the two direct microscopic reductions while explicitly preserving the open unbounded classification.
4. **Theoretical Computer Science.** A broad fallback for the combination of formal language design, counting complexity, and finite algorithms.

## Before submission

- Replace “Anonymous Authors” with names, affiliations, ORCIDs, funding, and acknowledgments.
- Obtain independent line-by-line reviews of the directed cycle-cover compiler, the forced-edge counting source, and the low-string signed-tangle argument.
- Decide whether the signed-tangle proof details belong in the main article or a technical appendix, without weakening the visible `n>=4` open status.
- Archive the exact release and add its DOI to the data-and-code statement.
- Check the selected journal's current template and disclosure policy at submission time.

This recommendation is based on journal scope as reviewed on 14 August 2026; it is not a claim of acceptance likelihood.

The manuscript must never be described as solving every Knotwang problem. The existentially unbounded language is only known to be NP-hard and recursively enumerable; its NP upper bound and r.e.-hardness/r.e.-completeness remain open.
